3DFusion: Lifting 2-D Diffusion Priors into an O-Voxel Interior

Two or three photographs per family cannot supervise a volume. The method: for every cut plane with no photograph, warp a depth-assigned reference into the silhouette and let one released single-image diffusion re-generate it — pinning only the rind, a light SDEdit that keeps the input’s structure — so the variation rides on the input, not the seed; then write the swept faces back into one 3-D field.

The base O-Voxel paper The splatted pipeline

One diffusion, constrained at generation: the input drives the cut face

The lift below shares one field across every plane, which is what makes it consistent and also what caps its detail: a single 8-D latent per cell cannot be sharp on every unseen depth at once. An earlier version of this page reached photographic detail by overfitting one diffusion per plane — a full 6000-step fit toward a warped reference, generated from noise with the supervised cells and background hard-pinned. It was sharp, but every seed produced the same face. The method below is what replaced it: one released single-image diffusion, no per-plane training, constrained only at sampling, with the variation moved onto the axis that actually carries it — the input.

Where a single-image diffusion’s variety actually lives

SinDiffusion has a patch-scale receptive field (no attention, no bottleneck), so it resamples an image’s local statistics into new layouts. Sampled unconditionally on our orange it is genuinely diverse. But that diversity is almost entirely global layout — where the silhouette sits, the big colour blocks — and our task fixes the silhouette and boundary by construction. Measuring the per-seed standard deviation in the flesh region on one plane makes the collapse quantitative:

regimeflesh stdoutcome
unconditional (unconstrained)0.130diverse, consistent texture
per-instance overfit0.030every seed identical

masked resampling (RePaint)0.011even lower

Pinning only a thin rind ring — the outpainting regime, a large free interior — does not rescue it either (std \(0.021\), and the interior loses all structure). The conclusion is that seed diversity is not a missing knob under these constraints; the correct source of variation is a different reference per plane, which is also what makes the depth sweep change with depth.

The generator: rind-only pin, low-strength SDEdit

For a plane with normal \(n\) and offset \(d\), rasterise the flesh silhouette \(f\) from the O-Voxel shell. A depth-assigned reference photograph is affine-warped to fill \(f\), giving the input image \(x_0\in[-1,1]^{3\times H\times W}\). The only pinned region is a thin rind ring plus the white background,

$$k \;=\; \big(f \wedge \neg\,\mathrm{erode}(f)\big)\;\vee\;(1-f),$$

and the interior — segments, juice vesicles, columella — is left entirely to the model. Generation is SDEdit: noise the input to a fraction \(\rho\) of the schedule and denoise from there, re-pinning the ring after every reverse step,

$$x_{\rho T}=\sqrt{\bar\alpha_{\rho T}}\,x_0+\sqrt{1-\bar\alpha_{\rho T}}\,\epsilon,\qquad x_{t-1}=k\odot\big(\sqrt{\bar\alpha_{t}}\,x_0+\sqrt{1-\bar\alpha_{t}}\,\epsilon_t\big)+(1-k)\odot\Phi(x_t,t),$$

with \(\Phi\) one unconditional DDPM reverse step. The strength \(\rho\) is the whole trade-off: at \(\rho\!\approx\!0.55\) the model washes the interior to a canonical smooth blob and the input stops mattering; at \(\rho\!\approx\!0.25\) the input’s structure survives, the diffusion only harmonises it into this plane’s silhouette and rind, and different references give visibly different, sharp cut faces. There is no per-plane training: a face is generated in a second.

The columella is geometry, carried by the input

The pith is a thin core along the fruit’s axis; whether a plane shows it is not a texture choice but an intersection. With a core cylinder of radius \(r_c\!\approx\!0.13\,R\) about the axis, a longitudinal plane at perpendicular offset \(s\) meets it over a band of half-width

$$w(s)=\sqrt{\,r_c^{2}-s^{2}\,}\ \ (\,|s|Rather than pin a columella, we crop the reference’s own pith to that width before warping: central planes carry a full pith, off-centre planes carry a thinner one, and beyond the core the input is de-pithed so the model grows none. The gating rides entirely on the input, which suits a patch-local model — it paints a columella only where its neighbourhood already looks like one.

From planes to one field

Each generated face \(r_p\) is splatted back over a slab of \(n_{\text{sub}}=5\) sub-planes at offsets \(o\in[-\delta,\delta]\), \(\delta=2\) cells; a cell’s colour is the mean of every reconstructed pixel that projects into it,

$$C_i=\frac{1}{|\mathcal P_i|}\sum_{(o,u)\in\mathcal P_i} r_{o}(u),\qquad \mathcal P_i=\big\{(o,u): \pi_{\,n,\,d+o}(u)=i,\ f(u)>0\big\},$$

with \(\pi\) the plane-to-cell projection. Twelve planes fill the field in about three seconds total; that field is what the video renders (slab, thickness \(2.82\) cells).

Two limits are honest and open. The write-back into the discrete lattice, plus the slab integration, softens faces that were sharp in 2-D — the same voxel-storage cost the shared lift pays; keeping the 2-D sharpness means generating each frame at render time instead of storing it. And the core is thin, so only the one or two most central planes carry a columella; the depth gating is correct but visually sparse.

The shared-field lift, for comparison

From left to right, four lifts of the same targets into the same field. The sparse lift is sharp on the six supervised depths but the sweep flickers between them; the dense lift fills every depth but the independent per-plane generations disagree and collapse to one texture, and the numbers regress. The last is the current method: one global flesh colour, a normal-direction smoothness that fills the depth gaps, a Laplacian that removes the lattice grid where no photograph constrains it, and a slab render for the face. The rest of this note is what each of those terms is and why.

The problem

The base pipeline fits a two-level occupancy lattice with an 8-D latent per cell, supervised by 1–12 cross-section photographs of different specimens. On planes those photographs reach, the fit is faithful; on planes they do not, the interior is a plausible average with no local detail. A held-out longitudinal cut of the orange scores 0.1942 in DreamSim against photographs of a real cut; a supervised one scores 0.0970.

The photographs are what they are: three per family, taken of different oranges, at unknown poses. They cannot themselves supervise every plane. But a 2-D diffusion model can be trained on those three photographs alone, and then used to write a target on planes that have none. Four stages:

Four stages

Stage 1: the fitted 3-D field. This is the base pipeline; nothing new. One 8-D latent per coarse cell, one shared decoder, the interior optimised against the photographs whose planes we know.

Stage 2: a 2-D diffusion model of a good cut face. SinDiffusion architecture, patch-scale receptive field, trained on the supervised longitudinal photographs of the orange — the three of them. 12,000 steps, four L40s. The held-out three (or_long_01/03/06) are never touched here or anywhere in what follows; they are the only thing left to measure against.

Stage 3: writing a target on a plane with no photograph (SDEdit). For each held-out plane, render the plane from the Stage 1 field, noise the render partway back under the diffusion schedule, then denoise under the Stage 2 model. The starting point is our render rather than pure noise, so the target keeps this object’s own layout and gains the statistics the render is missing. The peel is not refined: a flesh mask is derived from render_section(exterior=False), and at every reverse step everything outside that mask is pinned to the render diffused to that step. The shell we already have is the shell the target keeps.

Stage 4: lifting the targets into the one 3-D field. Only the per-cell latent moves; the shared decoder is frozen, so the supervised planes are not disturbed through it. Write \(R(n,d)\) for the flesh render of the field on the plane with unit normal \(n\) and offset \(d\), \(T_t\) for the target on plane \(t\), and \(M_t\) for its flesh mask. The field \(f=\{f_i\}\) (one 8-D latent per interior cell \(i\)) minimises

$$\mathcal{L}(f)=\underbrace{\tfrac{1}{|\mathcal T|}\sum_{t\in\mathcal T}\big\lVert R(n_t,d_t)-T_t\big\rVert^2_{M_t}}_{\text{data (fit every plane)}}\;+\;\lambda_a\lVert f-f^{0}\rVert^2\;+\;\lambda_n\,\mathcal L_{\mathrm{nTV}}\;+\;\lambda_\ell\,\mathcal L_{\mathrm{lap}},$$

anchored to the Stage 1 latent \(f^{0}\), and after every step each cell is norm-clamped, \(\lVert f_i\rVert\le\kappa\lVert f^{0}_i\rVert\), so a runaway cell cannot paint a bright speck. One field renders every target at once, so a texture plausible on one plane but disagreeing with the others cannot survive — that is what a single 2-D generation cannot supply. The colour of the targets, and the two regularisers \(\mathcal L_{\mathrm{nTV}}\) and \(\mathcal L_{\mathrm{lap}}\), are what turn a consistent-but-striped fit into a clean one; each is one measured decision.

The target colour is one global flesh distribution, not the render’s

The 2-D model is a strong generator of structure (columella, fibre, peel) but an unreliable one of colour: it collapses any input toward its single training image’s palette, and on transverse faces its flesh came out \(+0.07\) too bright, which lifted into bright horizontal bands in the longitudinal sweep. So the target keeps only the high-frequency luma of the generation and takes its colour from one global flesh distribution \(\bar c=(\bar Y,\overline{Cb},\overline{Cr})\), the mean over both families’ photograph flesh. In \(YCbCr\), with \(G\!*\) a flesh-masked low-pass and subscripts \(g,r\) for the generation and the render,

$$Y=\underbrace{(Y_g-G\!*\,Y_g)}_{\text{hi-freq detail}}+\underbrace{\big(G\!*\,Y_r-\overline{Y_r}\big)+\bar Y}_{\text{shading shape, global mean}},\qquad (Cb,Cr)=(Cb_r,Cr_r)-\overline{(Cb_r,Cr_r)}+(\overline{Cb},\overline{Cr}).$$

Every plane of the object now shares one colour distribution, so the sweep has no colour bands, and the distribution is the photograph’s rather than the duller render’s — the held-out score confirmed the render was the wrong reference, not the generation.

A normal-direction smoothness fills the gaps between supervised depths

Only the cells a target plane passes through are updated; between two supervised depths the interior stays at the Stage 1 average, so a sweep reads as sharp–smooth–sharp and the eye sees a stripe. A first-order smoothness along each held plane’s own normal — a finite difference by one step \(\Delta\) — pushes that plane’s detail into the neighbouring depth instead of leaving an island:

$$\mathcal L_{\mathrm{nTV}}=\sum_{h\in\mathcal H}\big\lVert R(n_h,\,d_h+\Delta)-R(n_h,\,d_h)\big\rVert^2_{M_h},\qquad \Delta\approx1.5\,\text{cells}.$$

It is applied on the held-out planes only. On the supervised planes it would drag the anchors toward their duller neighbours; kept off them, the supervised guardrails hold.

A gap-only Laplacian removes the lattice grid where no photograph constrains it

The interior is sampled trilinearly, so it is \(C^0\); but the derivative still jumps at every coarse-cell boundary, and that \(C^1\) kink renders as a faint cross-hatch grid. A discrete Laplacian on the decoded colour \(z_i=g(f_i)\) over the lattice graph forces \(C^1\) continuity (it penalises curvature, so linear gradients — the columella, the shading — survive). It is weighted to zero within one cell of any target plane, so it smooths only the unobserved gap cells and never the scored faces:

$$\mathcal L_{\mathrm{lap}}=\frac{\sum_i w_i\,\big\lVert\sum_{j\in\mathcal N(i)}(z_j-z_i)\big\rVert^2}{\sum_i w_i},\qquad w_i=1-\max_{p\in\mathcal T}\mathbf 1\big[\,|x_i\!\cdot n_p+d_p|The face is a slab, not a zero-thickness plane

The pipeline’s real cut face is not a mathematical plane but an integral over a slab of half-width \(\delta\) (measured at \(2.82\) coarse cells for this orange); a zero-thickness section aliases the lattice into vertical depth-sampling stripes. Rendering the sweep as the slab average low-passes them away:

$$R_{\mathrm{slab}}(n,d)=\frac1K\sum_{k=1}^{K}R\big(n,\,d+o_k\big),\qquad o_k\in[-\delta,\delta].$$

Anti-aliasing against fidelity: the lattice’s Pareto edge

The cross-hatch grid is a \(C^1\) kink at every cell boundary. The Laplacian \(\mathcal L_{\mathrm{lap}}\) removes it — but on a discrete lattice, removing it and keeping the supervised planes sharp pull against each other, because the residual grid lives in the same low-gradient flesh that carries the photograph’s micro-texture. A global Laplacian strong enough to erase the grid smears the anchors (\(+0.047\) on the supervised longitudinal); weighting it to zero near every target plane (the current method) holds the anchors at the seed floor but only reduces the grid, because half the volume lies within a cell of some plane and keeps its grid. Making the weight edge-aware (relax where \(\lVert\nabla z^{0}\rVert\) is large) and the mask a soft Gaussian does not escape the wall: the grid and the micro-texture overlap in gradient space, so no weight separates them. The gap-only point plus a slab render is the practical optimum of this representation; a fully grid-free interior needs a different one.

Why the grid is a representation limit, and what is not

Two things were separated by replacing the per-cell latent with a continuous tri-plane interior — three feature planes \(T_{xy},T_{yz},T_{zx}\in\mathbb R^{C\times R\times R}\) and a shallow decoder, queried by trilinear projection at the fragment’s world position \(x\): \(z(x)=g\big([\,T_{xy}(x_{xy});T_{yz}(x_{yz});T_{zx}(x_{zx})\,]\big)\).

Fit to the Stage 1 field it renders with no grid at all — a continuous field has no cell boundary, so no \(C^1\) kink, at no smoothing cost. That is the proof that the grid is a property of the discrete lattice, not of the lift. But lifting the tri-plane to the targets does not beat the lattice: its global continuity couples nearby planes, so fitting the held-out detail drags the supervised planes (\(+0.05\) to \(+0.09\) on the supervised longitudinal), and its capacity blurs the held detail the per-cell latent kept sharp (\(-0.005\) against the lattice’s \(-0.026\)).

What that isolates is the real ceiling. When the supervised planes are not frozen and every plane is asked to match its own generated target, the fit residual triples and the guardrails regress — on the tri-plane and on the dense lattice alike. The residual blur is not the representation; it is that the targets are generated independently, one plane at a time, and independent 2-D generations do not agree in 3-D. A shared field can only average their disagreement. Removing it needs a target that is 3-D-consistent by construction — a generative model over the volume (a true 3-D or tri-plane diffusion), which needs many fitted volumes to train and is left as the next stage. The current method is the best that the 2-D prior and this representation admit: sharp on the planes a photograph reaches, clean between them, and honest about the one thing it cannot yet buy.

Stage 3 sweep: how far back to noise

SDEdit’s single knob is the fraction of the schedule the render is noised through before being denoised. Higher values give the model more room to make the target look right; lower values keep it close to the render. Whether keeping the shell locked matters is the same question, so both are swept together. DreamSim against the same three held-out photographs, lower is better.

What each stage was tried against, and rejected

Two paths were built and measured before the current one; both failed, and the failures are informative about what makes Stage 3 work.

3-D diffusion over the latent volume. A UNet3D was trained on random 323 crops of the fitted latent volume, then sampled from noise inside a band around the held-out plane while every cell outside the band was pinned to Stage 1. DreamSim: 0.2180 → 0.3237 (+52%). Two mistakes made this worse than doing nothing. The predicted noise at the full-volume scale never fell below 0.96 of unit even at t = 100 — the model was trained on 323 where 100% of positions carry the object, and asked to sample on 117×128×120 where 57% are exactly zero; GroupNorm reads different statistics on the two, so it never realises the input is nearly clean. Tiling at the training size fixes that. The other one: clip_denoised was off because the latent is not in [−1, 1], but the cosine schedule multiplies noise-prediction errors by √(1/ā) = 300+ at t = 999; the samples ended 158 times larger than the fit they started from. A bound at the latent’s own scale is what was needed.

Latent inpainting of a band around each plane. Same 3-D model, samples used to overwrite the fitted cells within ±4 coarse cells of the held-out plane. Discarded on grounds that pre-dated the failure: the band the ±4 rule selects is not the set of cells without supervision. Cells within four of a held-out longitudinal plane are still hit by transverse photographs, so the ablation was replacing photograph-supervised cells with a weak model’s guess. What is unobserved is defined by the union of the plane families’ slabs, not by proximity to any one plane.

The path that works is not the ambitious one. Diffusion runs on the 2-D face, where a model that has to know the object at all can be trained on three photographs and produces a target that is judged by other photographs of the same object. The lift into 3-D is a projected update to the field, not a generation.

Why one field, not six 2-D targets

Stage 4 could be replaced by six independent updates — overwrite the latent at each plane with the target for that plane. It is not, because the lattice is the same lattice at every plane, and a cell touched by two held-out planes gets one value. Two targets on the two planes at that cell must agree, or one of them will be pulled towards the other and both towards the field. In practice the flesh masks of six planes overlap over most of the interior, and the joint optimisation is what enforces the agreement. A single 2-D generation cannot do that.

What is not claimed

The 2-D model is trained on this object’s own supervised photographs, and the target it writes on a held-out plane is a plausible cut face of this specimen — not a ground truth. DreamSim measures perceptual distance to real photographs of a similar cut, and it goes down; SSIM, LPIPS and a per-plane pixel loss are not reported here because we do not think they are the right measurement. The lift is a modest change to a fit that already exists; it is not a generative model of oranges.

The Stage 4 lift moves the guardrail by 0.008, which is inside the seed repeat, but that has been measured on one object and one held-out set. Applying this to the other six objects, and to transverse planes, is what comes next. The base O-Voxel measurements do not depend on any of this — if Stage 4 turns out to be object-specific or unstable, the base pipeline is unchanged.
