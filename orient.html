Which way is up

Every object on a turntable, with the lattice's own axes drawn through it

O‑Voxel columnPaper

Drag to turn the object; drag up and down to change elevation. The three arrows are the lattice's axes through the object's centre — not a camera's, and not the physics config's, so what they point at is where the data actually is. Say which one is the object's top and which is its front, and the answer goes into objects/<obj>.conf beside the references it applies to.

This exists because the automatic version could not be defended. Registering a reference to the shell by trying its four flips only decides the question where the reference and the shell already agree in shape, which is not where it is needed: it answered for the orange and the doughnut and returned margins under 3% for everything else, and a flip is the wrong transform for a stem in the wrong place anyway.
